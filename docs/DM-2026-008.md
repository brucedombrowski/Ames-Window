# DM-2026-008: Human is a named role, and Major decisions follow a set form

| | |
|---|---|
| Date | 2026-11-11 |
| Status | Directed by the Human |
| Affects | `docs/configuration-management.md`; issues #17 to #20 |

## Purpose

[DM-2026-007](DM-2026-007.md) let AI agents hold the reviewing roles and left Major decisions
with "the owner". That word describes who holds the repository, not what
they do in the process. The owner asked for two things: that Human be a
role in its own right, and that a decision put to the Human, a risk
acceptance for instance, carry a response from the AI Technical Lead to the
question as first asked.

## Options considered

1. **Leave it as "the owner".** No change. The process then has titled roles
   for every participant but one, and nothing says what a Major decision
   looks like on the record.
2. **Name the role and fix the form of a Major decision.** One role only a
   person can hold, and a short procedure: a question, an agent's advice, the
   Human's decision, the record updated.
3. **Require the agent's advice before the Human may decide.** Stricter, and
   it would make the Human wait on an agent, which is the opposite of what
   [DM-2026-007](DM-2026-007.md) set out to do.

## Decision

Option 2.

**Human** is a role. It is held by the repository owner and is the one role
an AI agent cannot hold. The Human observes, directs, and decides what the
plan calls Major: the charter, the change classes, releases, and whether to
accept a known weakness or risk. The Human is the control board for this
repository.

This refines [DM-2026-007](DM-2026-007.md) and reverses none of it.

A Major decision is made on an issue, in this form:

1. **Question.** Any role may open the issue. It states what is asked and
   everything needed to decide it. For a risk: the statement, the ratings before and after
   controls, the controls relied on, why it is carried, and when it is
   reviewed again.
2. **Technical Lead response.** An AI agent holding the title Technical Lead
   replies on the issue with its own assessment of the question as asked and
   a recommendation. It must be a different agent from the one that wrote
   the issue, and it checks the question's claims against the repository
   rather than repeating them. It is advice: the Human may decide against it,
   and the decision stands.
3. **Decision.** The Human replies in their own words: concur, do not concur,
   or something else to be done.
4. **Record.** An agent brings the affected document into line with the
   decision, whichever way it went, citing the issue. The issue is closed
   once the response, the decision and the updated record all exist.

Steps 2 and 3 may come in either order. The Human is never made to wait: if
they decide first, the Technical Lead's response still goes on the record,
and says plainly if it found something the decision did not have in front of
it. The Human may then let the decision stand or change it, on the same
issue.

One exception, and its limit. A direction the Human gives in a working
session is sufficient for a Major change, and is recorded in the change that
carries it out. That does not extend to accepting a risk or a known
weakness, which always takes the issue form above, because that is the
decision whose basis most needs to be on the record.

## Rationale

- NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 CM-3 asks for change decisions to be made by a
  named authority and recorded with their basis. The issue holds the
  question, the advice, the decision and the link to the change, in one
  place and in order.
- NIST SP 800-37 Rev. 2 gives risk acceptance to an accountable official,
  the authorizing official, informed by assessment. An agent can assess. It cannot be accountable, so the role
  that accepts is the one an agent cannot hold.
- Advice that only restates the question is worth nothing, which is why the
  response is written by a different agent from the one that wrote the
  issue, and must check the claims.

## Consequences and known limits

- Agents act through the Human's GitHub account, so GitHub itself shows the
  same author on an agent's comment and the Human's. The convention that
  tells them apart: every agent comment ends with a generated-by line; the
  Human's comments do not. This is a convention, not a control. Separate accounts for agents would make it one.
- The first decisions made this way are the risk acceptances in issues #17
  to #20. The risk register they refer to is not yet on `main`; it arrives
  with pull request #12. Once it does, its risk owners are roles from this
  plan, and a risk whose response is `accept` is owned by the Human.
- "Release authority", used in the plan and the release script, means the
  Human.
